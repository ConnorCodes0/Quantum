# Quantum

**Quantum** is a personal design system and component library that I am building to explore the principles behind creating scalable, maintainable and reusable user interfaces.

The project builds on the experience I gained working with enterprise design systems during my industry placement, particularly around **Atomic Design, component reusability, consistency and maintainability**.

> **Status:** Quantum is currently under active development. Components, APIs and project structure may change as the system evolves.

## Why Quantum?

Modern applications can quickly become difficult to maintain when interface elements are created independently for individual features.

Quantum is my way of exploring a more systematic approach.

Rather than treating each page as an isolated interface, Quantum is being developed around a collection of reusable components that can be combined to create increasingly complex interfaces while maintaining consistent behaviour and visual design.

The project gives me an environment to experiment with the challenges involved in building a design system that can grow without becoming difficult to understand or maintain.

## Core Principles

### Atomic Design

Quantum follows principles inspired by **Atomic Design**, where interfaces are constructed from smaller reusable elements.

The aim is to build from simple components into increasingly complex structures while avoiding unnecessary duplication.

This allows changes to fundamental components to propagate throughout the wider system and encourages consistency between interfaces.

### Reusability

Components are designed to solve recurring interface problems rather than being tightly coupled to one particular page.

Where appropriate, components should be configurable and composable so that the same foundations can support different use cases.

### Maintainability

A design system is only useful if developers can understand and modify it.

As Quantum develops, I am focusing on keeping components clearly structured, reducing duplication and maintaining separation between different responsibilities.

### Scalability

Quantum is intended to grow beyond a small collection of UI components.

The architecture is therefore being developed with future expansion in mind, considering how components can remain organised and predictable as the library becomes larger.

### Consistency

Shared components provide a single foundation for common interface patterns.

The goal is to make visual and behavioural consistency a property of the system rather than something that has to be recreated manually for every page.

## The Quantum Creator

A longer-term goal for the project is the **Quantum Creator**.

The Creator is intended to provide an interface where components available within Quantum can be selected, configured and combined to construct complete pages.

The idea is to move from:

**Components → Compositions → Complete Interfaces**

Rather than manually recreating common interface structures, the Creator will explore how a design system can provide the building blocks for assembling interfaces while retaining the constraints and consistency established by the underlying component library.

This part of Quantum is still conceptual and will evolve alongside the component system.

## Background

The idea for Quantum grew from my experience working as a **UX Developer during my industry placement**, where I gained practical experience working with reusable components, design systems and Atomic Design principles within an enterprise environment.

That experience changed how I approached frontend development. Instead of thinking only about how to implement an individual interface, I became increasingly interested in questions such as:

- Can this component be reused?
- How will this approach scale?
- What happens when the design needs to change?
- How can duplication be reduced?
- How do we maintain consistency across a large application?
- Will another developer be able to understand and extend this?

Quantum is an opportunity to continue exploring those questions independently and put the principles I learned into practice within a system I can develop from the ground up.
